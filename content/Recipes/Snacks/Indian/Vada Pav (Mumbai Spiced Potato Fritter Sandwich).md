## Ingredients

### Green Chutney
- 1 to 1 1/2 cups coriander leaves
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds (jeera)
- 2 hot green chilies (adjust to taste)
- 2 small garlic cloves, peeled
- 1 to 2 teaspoons lemon juice (adjust to taste)
- 1 to 2 tablespoons water (only as needed)
- 1/4 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste)
- 1 to 2 tablespoons dried coconut (or roasted peanuts, fried gram, or a slice of white bread; optional)

### Dry Garlic Chutney
- 6 to 8 garlic cloves
- 6 to 10 dried red chilies (Byadgi, Kashmiri, or any less spicy kind)
- 1/2 to 1 cup dried coconut (copra) (use 1/2 cup for more garlic flavor)
- 1 tablespoon peanuts (optional)
- 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon Kashmiri red chili powder (optional, for color)

### Vada
- 3 medium potatoes (about 2 1/4 cups crumbled)
- 2 to 3 green chilies (adjust to taste)
- 3/4 inch ginger, peeled
- 2 to 3 cloves garlic, peeled
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon mustard seeds
- 2 pinches hing (asafoetida)
- 1 sprig curry leaves
- 1/4 teaspoon turmeric
- 1/8 teaspoon red chili powder (optional)
- 1/2 to 3/4 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste)
- 3 tablespoons coriander leaves, finely chopped

### Vada Batter
- 3/4 cup gram flour (besan)
- 2 to 3 tablespoons rice flour (or cornstarch)
- 1/4 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste)
- 1 pinch asafoetida (hing)
- Tiny pinch baking soda (optional)
- 1 pinch turmeric
- 1/8 teaspoon red chili powder (optional)
- oil for deep frying

### For Serving
- 1/4 cup tamarind chutney (optional)
- 7 to 8 pav
- 7 to 8 green chilies, deseeded

## Instructions

1. Green chutney: rinse the coriander well and drain. Blend with the remaining chutney ingredients to a thick paste, adding water 1 tablespoon at a time. Adjust salt, lemon, and chili; keep it thick so the pav does not go soggy. Refrigerate in a clean jar until needed.
2. Garlic chutney: dry roast the peeled garlic 2 to 3 minutes. Add the red chilies and roast until crisp; cool on a plate. Warm the coconut in the same pan just until hot; cool. If using peanuts, dry roast over medium until golden.
3. Grind the chilies with the chili powder and salt, then add the garlic and pulse, then the coconut and peanuts. Grind coarsely and set aside.
4. Vada filling: boil the potatoes until just fork tender (firm, not mushy); cool and crumble lightly. Pound the green chilies, garlic, and ginger to a coarse paste.
5. Heat 1 tablespoon oil over low heat. Add the mustard seeds; when they crackle, add the curry leaves and fry until crisp. Add the chili-ginger-garlic paste and fry until fragrant.
6. Add the hing, salt, turmeric, and chili powder, then the potatoes and coriander. Turn off the heat and mix well. Cool, then shape into 7 to 8 balls.
7. Batter: heat oil for deep frying in a kadai. Mix the gram flour, rice flour, salt, hing, turmeric, and chili powder. Add about 4 tablespoons water and mix to a thick, lump-free batter, adding more water as needed until neither too thick nor too thin. When the oil is hot, mix in the baking soda if using.
8. Test the oil with a drop of batter: it should sizzle and rise without browning. Lower the heat to medium.
9. Dip each potato ball in batter and slide into the oil, 3 to 5 at a time. Leave undisturbed 2 minutes until firm, then stir gently and fry until golden. Drain on a rack.
10. Dry and deseed the green chilies and fry them lightly in the same oil in a strainer.
11. Slit each pav, leaving one side attached. Spread green chutney inside, sprinkle with garlic chutney, and add tamarind chutney if you like. Add a vada (flattened first if you prefer). Serve with the fried chilies and extra chutneys.
