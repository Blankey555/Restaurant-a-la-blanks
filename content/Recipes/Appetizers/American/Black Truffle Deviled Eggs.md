# Black Truffle Deviled Eggs

*Truffle-aromatized eggs with truffle-pureed filling and frozen truffle shavings on top.*

**Yield:** 60 halves (30 eggs)
**Total Time:** 2 to 3 days aromatizing plus 1.5 hours active

## Ingredients

### Eggs
- 30 large eggs
- Fresh black truffle, for aromatizing (the same 24 g truffle destined for the filling)

### Filling
- 24 g black winter truffle (reuse the aromatizing truffle; approximately 0.8 g per egg)
- 180 g mayonnaise, good quality
- 45 g Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp white pepper
- 1 tsp fine sea salt

### Garnish
- 20 g frozen black truffle, for grating
- 2 tbsp chives, finely sliced
- Flaky sea salt

---

## Instructions

**Aromatize the eggs**

1. Place the eggs in a sealed container with the fresh truffle 2 to 3 days before making the deviled eggs. Pack them snugly in a small container with the truffle unwrapped and loose to concentrate the aroma. Keep refrigerated. The aroma penetrates the porous shells and perfumes both white and yolk.

**Boil the eggs**

2. Place eggs in a single layer in a pot and cover with cold water by an inch. Bring to a rolling boil, then remove from heat, cover, and let sit for 11 minutes.
3. Transfer immediately to an ice bath and cool completely, about 15 minutes.

**Peel and halve**

4. Peel the eggs and slice in half lengthwise. Place the yolks in a bowl and arrange the whites on serving trays.

**Make the filling**

5. Finely grate the truffle on a microplane into the bowl with the yolks. Add mayonnaise, Dijon, white pepper, and salt. Mash and mix until completely smooth.
6. Taste and adjust seasoning. Slightly over-season the filling since the bland whites will dilute the flavor.

**Pipe and garnish**

7. Transfer filling to a piping bag fitted with a large star tip. Pipe into the egg white halves, mounding slightly above the rim.
8. Finely grate the frozen truffle over each egg using a microplane. Finish with chives and flaky salt.
9. Serve within a couple of hours for best texture and aroma.

---

## Notes
- The 11 minute method gives a fully set but not chalky yolk. Drop to 9 minutes for a jammier center, but piping becomes messier with a softer yolk.
- Skip vinegar and lemon juice in the filling. Acid fights truffle. The Dijon provides sufficient tang.
- The aromatizing step is optional but produces a noticeable difference. Two to three days gives deeply perfumed eggs; one day gives only a hint.
- Shell porosity varies egg to egg, so aromatizing results will be somewhat uneven. Thinner-shelled farm eggs absorb more than thick-shelled commercial eggs.
- Freezing pauses truffle ripening, so the frozen garnish truffle retains the profile it had on the day it was frozen.
- Grated truffle blended directly into the fat-rich filling achieves full flavor integration without heat. This is one of the most forgiving truffle applications.
