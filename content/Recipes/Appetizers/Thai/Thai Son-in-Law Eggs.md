## Ingredients

### Eggs
- 8 large eggs
### Marinade
- 3 large shallots, peeled and sliced thinly lengthwise
- 3 Scallions, thinly chopped
- 1/4 cup sesame oil
- 1/2 cup very finely chopped palm sugar, packed (or 1/4 cup brown sugar)
- 1/4 cup Thai fish sauce
- 2 tablespoons prepared tamarind pulp
- 3 tablespoons warm water

### Garnish
- 2 red jalapeño peppers or half a red bell pepper, deseeded and cut into slivers
- Fresh cilantro leaves
- Scallions
- Toasted Sesame Seeds

## Instructions

1. Soft-boil the eggs: lower them into boiling water and cook for 6 1/2 to 7 minutes. Transfer immediately to an ice bath, cool completely, and peel.
2. Chop the aromatics: slice the shallots thinly lengthwise, sliver the peppers, and chop the palm sugar as finely as possible so it dissolves.
3. Combine the marinade: stir the palm sugar into the warm water until mostly dissolved, then whisk in the fish sauce, tamarind pulp, and sesame oil. Fold in the sliced shallots.
4. Marinate: place the peeled eggs in a container just large enough to hold them, pour the marinade over, and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. Turn the eggs once or twice so they color evenly.
5. Serve halved, spooned with marinade and the now-pickled shallots, garnished with pepper slivers and cilantro.

## Notes

- The shallots soften and lightly pickle in the marinade, standing in for the traditional fried-shallot topping. If you want the classic crunch, crisp them in the sesame oil first, but the recipe works without it.
- Traditional son-in-law eggs are deep-fried before saucing. Skipping the fry trades the blistered skin for a cleaner, jammier egg with the drunk egg flavor profile
- Beyond 24 hours the whites can turn rubbery and the seasoning can get aggressive. Best between 4 and 24 hours.
- The marinade can be reused once for a second batch within a few days. Keep refrigerated.
