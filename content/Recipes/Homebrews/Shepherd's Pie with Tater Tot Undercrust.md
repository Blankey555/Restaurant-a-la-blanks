*A deeply savory shepherd's pie built on a Guinness-braised meat filling, a smashed tater tot undercrust, and a rich cheddar-mash top crust. The meat is cooked like boereg filling — onions, butter, low and slow — before braising.*

**Serves:** 6 to 8
**Time:** 2 hours

---

## Components

- Guinness-Braised Meat Filling
- Smashed Tater Tot Undercrust
- Cheddar Mashed Potato Top Crust

---

## Guinness-Braised Meat Filling

### Ingredients
- 1½ lb ground lamb (or beef)
- 1 large onion, finely diced
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 medium carrots, diced
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 bottle Guinness stout
- 1 cup beef or lamb broth
- 1 tbsp chili crisp
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- 1 tbsp flour or cornstarch slurry, if needed for thickening

### Instructions
1. Melt butter in a large heavy-bottomed pan or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add onions and cook low and slow, stirring occasionally, until deeply softened and beginning to caramelize, about 15 to 20 minutes. This is the boereg method — patience here builds the base flavor.
2. Add garlic and cook for another 2 minutes.
3. Increase heat to medium-high. Add the ground meat and cook, breaking it up, until browned and any moisture has evaporated.
4. Add carrots and stir to combine.
5. Add tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes, stirring to coat the meat.
6. Pour in the Guinness slowly, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Let it bubble and reduce for 5 minutes.
7. Add broth, chili crisp, bay leaves, rosemary, and thyme. Stir to combine.
8. Reduce heat to low and cook uncovered, allowing the liquid to absorb and cook down gradually, stirring occasionally, for 30 to 40 minutes. The filling should be moist but not soupy.
9. Add peas in the final 5 minutes of cooking.
10. If the liquid needs thickening, stir in a flour or cornstarch slurry and cook for a further 2 to 3 minutes until thickened.
11. Remove bay leaves and herb sprigs. Taste and adjust seasoning.

## Smashed Tater Tot Undercrust

### Ingredients
- 1 bag frozen tater tots (approximately 32 oz)
- Olive oil or cooking spray
- Salt

### Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 425°F.
2. Arrange tater tots in a single layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Bake for 25 to 30 minutes until very crispy and deeply golden.
3. Transfer immediately to the bottom of a greased baking dish and smash firmly with the bottom of a glass or a potato masher to form a dense, even crust layer.
4. Set aside while you prepare the remaining components.

---

## Cheddar Mashed Potato Top Crust

### Ingredients
- 2 lb russet or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cubed
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter (or browned butter for extra depth)
- 1 cup sharp cheddar, grated, plus more for topping
- 3 tbsp Greek yogurt
- Salt and white pepper, to taste
- Parmesan or additional cheddar, for topping

### Instructions
1. Boil potatoes in well-salted water until completely tender, about 20 minutes. Drain and return to the pot over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes to dry out.
2. While potatoes cook, warm cream in a small saucepan with rosemary and thyme over low heat for 10 minutes. Do not boil. Strain and discard the herbs.
3. Mash potatoes until smooth. Add herb-infused cream gradually and mix to incorporate.
4. Add butter, cheddar, and Greek yogurt. Mix until smooth and creamy. Season with salt and white pepper.

---

## Assembly and Baking

1. Preheat oven to 400°F.
2. Spread the smashed tater tot crust evenly across the bottom of the baking dish and press firmly.
3. Spoon the meat filling evenly over the tater tot crust.
4. Spread or pipe the mashed potato topping over the filling in an even layer, all the way to the edges to seal.
5. Top generously with grated cheddar, parmesan, or a mix of both.
6. Bake for 25 to 30 minutes until the top is golden and bubbling at the edges.
7. Rest for 10 minutes before serving.

---

## Notes
- The slow onion cook at the start is the foundation of the filling. Do not rush it.
- Guinness adds bitterness and depth. Add it slowly and let it cook down — the bitterness mellows significantly with time.
- Chili crisp adds background heat and umami rather than obvious spice. Adjust to taste.
- Browned butter in the mash adds a nutty complexity that complements the Guinness in the filling.
- Greek yogurt lightens the mash and adds a slight tang that cuts through the richness.
- The tater tot crust must be very crispy before smashing or it will become soggy under the filling.
- For a cleaner serving, refrigerate the assembled pie before baking so it sets up more firmly.
