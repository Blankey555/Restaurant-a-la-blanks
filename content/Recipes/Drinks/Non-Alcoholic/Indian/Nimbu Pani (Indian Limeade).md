## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp fresh lime juice (approximately 1 to 2 limes)
- 1 to 2 tsp sugar, or to taste
- ¼ tsp black salt (kala namak)
- ¼ tsp chaat masala
- Pinch of black pepper powder
- 1 cup cold water or chilled sparkling water
- Ice

---

## Instructions

1. Combine lime juice and sugar in a glass. Stir until the sugar dissolves completely.
2. Add black salt, chaat masala, and black pepper. Stir to combine.
3. Add ice and pour in cold water. Stir well.
4. Taste and adjust: more lime for tartness, more sugar for sweetness, more black salt for depth.
5. Serve immediately.

---

## Spiked Variations

### Vodka
- Add 1½ oz of a clean, neutral vodka.
- Reduce water to ¾ cup to maintain balance.
- A coconut vodka adds an unexpected but complementary sweetness.
- Increase sugar slightly if using a particularly dry vodka.

### Tequila
- Add 1½ oz blanco tequila. Reposado works well for a rounder, oakier result.
- Reduce water to ¾ cup.
- Increase lime juice by ½ tbsp to complement the agave character.
- Increase chaat masala by a pinch. Tequila can handle more spice than vodka.
- A salted rim made with black salt and chaat masala mixed together ties the cocktail together and makes for a striking presentation.

---

## Notes
- Black salt is essential to the character of this drink. It has a sulfurous, eggy quality that transforms nimbu pani from a simple limeade into something distinctly Indian. Do not substitute regular salt.
- Chaat masala already contains black salt and dried mango powder, so add additional black salt gradually and taste as you go.
- Sparkling water adds pleasant effervescence and works especially well in the spiked versions.
- For a large batch, mix lime juice, sugar, and spices as a concentrate and dilute and spike to order.
- Traditionally served at room temperature in some regions, but cold or iced is equally common.
