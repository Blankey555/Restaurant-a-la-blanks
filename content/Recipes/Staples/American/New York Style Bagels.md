## Ingredients

- 3 1/2 cups (455 grams) unbleached bread flour
- 1 cup plus 2 tablespoons (255 grams) lukewarm water, plus 2 to 3 quarts for boiling
- 2 1/2 tablespoons (49.5 grams) barley malt syrup or honey, divided
- 1 1/4 teaspoons (3.75 grams) active dry yeast or 1 teaspoon (3 grams) instant yeast
- 2 1/2 teaspoons (10.5 grams) kosher salt
- Olive oil or nonstick cooking spray
- 1 tablespoon (14 grams) baking soda
- Toppings such as sesame seeds, poppy seeds, coarse salt, onion flakes, or garlic flakes

## Instructions

1. In a stand mixer with the dough hook, combine the flour, lukewarm water, 1 tablespoon malt syrup, and yeast. Knead on low 2 to 3 minutes into a shaggy dough. Cover with a damp towel and rest 10 minutes.
2. Add the salt and knead on medium 7 to 10 minutes, until elastic, pulling away from the bowl, and no longer sticky. (By hand: mix with your fingers, rest 10 minutes, add salt, and knead on a lightly floured counter 10 to 15 minutes.)
3. Shape into a ball, place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let rise 1 hour at room temperature or up to 4 hours in the fridge, until doubled. Lightly oil a parchment-lined baking sheet.
4. Weigh the dough and divide into 6 equal pieces. Roll each into a taut ball and rest, covered, 10 minutes.
5. Poke a hole through the center of each ball and gently stretch it to about 2 inches across. Set on the baking sheet.
6. Brush or spray with oil, cover with plastic, and refrigerate 8 to 12 hours (overnight).
7. Take the bagels out about 1 hour before baking and heat the oven to 500°F.
8. Fill a wide pot with about 4 inches of water, bring to a rolling boil, then lower to a simmer. Stir in the remaining 1 1/2 tablespoons malt syrup and the baking soda.
9. Simmer the bagels 3 to 4 at a time, 1 minute per side. Return to the sheet and sprinkle with toppings while wet.
10. Bake about 20 minutes, rotating halfway, until deep brown and crisp. If the bottoms brown too fast, slide a second baking sheet underneath.
11. Cool completely before slicing.

## Notes

- From KQED Bay Area Bites (2014); recovered from a 2015 Internet Archive snapshot.
- The dough can be doubled for 12 bagels (use two baking sheets); the boiling bath does not need doubling.
- Barley malt syrup is sold at high-end grocers or brewing supply stores; honey works instead.
- Best the day they are made; they keep a couple of days in an airtight container and are best toasted after that.
