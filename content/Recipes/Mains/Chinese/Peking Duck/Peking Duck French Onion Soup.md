*A Chinese-inflected French onion soup built on rendered duck fat, homemade duck stock, and Shaoxing wine. Five spice runs through the entire cook. Finished with crusty bread and Gruyère.*

**Serves:** 4
**Active Time:** 1 hour
**Total Time:** 3 to 4 hours (including stock)

---

## Components

- Duck Stock
- Caramelized Onion
- Bread Crust and Gruyère

---

## Duck Stock

### Ingredients
- Roasted Peking duck carcass and any reserved bones
- Duck meat scraps
- 1 onion, halved
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 inch piece fresh ginger, sliced
- 2 star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 2 tbsp Shaoxing rice wine
- 1 tbsp Chinese red vinegar (Zhenjiang/Chinkiang)
- Cold water to cover
- Salt to taste

### Instructions
1. Roast the duck carcass and bones at 425°F for 20 to 25 minutes until deeply browned.
2. Transfer to a large stockpot. Add onion, garlic, ginger, star anise, cinnamon, Shaoxing wine, and red vinegar. Cover with cold water.
3. Bring to a boil, skim off any foam, then reduce to a low simmer.
4. Simmer uncovered for 3 to 4 hours until the stock is deeply flavored and has reduced by about a third.
5. Strain through a fine mesh sieve. Season lightly with salt. Set aside.

---

## Caramelized Onion Soup

### Ingredients
- ½ cup rendered Peking duck fat (substitute unsalted butter if needed)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 4 cups onions, thinly sliced
- 5 cups duck stock
- 3 tbsp Shaoxing rice wine, divided
- 1 tbsp Chinese red vinegar
- ¼ tsp five spice powder
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- Salt and white pepper, to taste

### Optional Additions
- 1 to 2 packets unflavored gelatin, bloomed in 2 tbsp cold water and stirred in at the end of simmering, for added body and a silkier texture
- 2 tbsp duck or veal demi-glace, stirred in with the stock, for deeper umami and richness

### Instructions
1. Heat duck fat and olive oil together in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-low heat.
2. Add sliced onions and cook low and slow, stirring occasionally, for 45 to 60 minutes until deeply golden and caramelized. Do not rush this step. The onions should be sweet, jammy, and dark amber.
3. Add 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine and the red vinegar. Stir to deglaze, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot. Cook for 2 minutes until the liquid absorbs.
4. Add duck stock, five spice powder, and thyme. Add demi-glace here if using. Season with salt and white pepper.
5. Simmer uncovered for 30 minutes.
6. If using gelatin, stir the bloomed gelatin into the hot soup in the final 5 minutes of simmering.
7. Just before serving, stir in the remaining 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine off the heat to preserve its aromatic quality.
8. Taste and adjust seasoning. Add a splash more red vinegar if the soup needs brightness.

---

## Bread Crust and Gruyère

### Ingredients
- 4 thick slices crusty bread (sourdough or baguette preferred)
- Unsalted butter, for toasting
- 1½ cups Gruyère, grated

### Instructions
1. Butter the bread slices generously and toast in a pan or under the broiler until very firm and golden on both sides. The bread must be sturdy enough to support the cheese without dissolving into the soup.
2. Preheat the broiler.
3. Ladle hot soup into oven-safe bowls. Float a slice of toasted bread on top of each.
4. Cover generously with grated Gruyère.
5. Place under the broiler for 2 to 4 minutes until the cheese is bubbling and deeply golden. Watch closely.
6. Serve immediately.

---

## Optional Substitutions

**Gluten-Free**
- Substitute GF bread for the crusty bread. Double toast until very firm and crouton-like so it holds up under the cheese and broiler without going soggy.
- Substitute pale dry sherry for the Shaoxing wine in equal quantities. Add it at the same stages as directed.

**Duck Fat on Bread**
- Substitute rendered duck fat for the butter when toasting the bread. Brush generously on both sides before toasting. Adds a rich, roasted depth that ties the bread directly into the soup.

---

## Notes
- Rendered Peking duck fat is the key building block. Save every drop from the roasting pan.
- Five spice should be present but subtle. It should make someone ask what is different rather than announcing itself.
- Shaoxing wine added at the end is intentional. Its aromatic compounds bloom best off the heat.
- Chinese red vinegar adds depth and slight sweetness rather than sharp acidity. Do not substitute with rice vinegar.
- The stock can be made a day ahead and refrigerated. Skim the solidified fat from the surface before reheating and save it alongside the rendered roasting fat.
- Gelatin is especially useful if the duck stock is lean. It replicates the body that a collagen-rich stock or demi-glace provides naturally.
