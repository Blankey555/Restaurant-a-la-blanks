*A Chinese-inflected French onion soup built on rendered duck fat, homemade duck stock, and Shaoxing wine. Five spice runs through the entire cook. Finished with crusty bread and Gruyère.*

**Serves:** 4
**Active Time:** 1 hour
**Total Time:** 3 to 12 hours depending on how long you let it go

---

## Components

- Duck Stock
- Caramelized Onion Soup
- Bread Crust and Gruyère

---

## Duck Stock

### Ingredients
- Roasted Peking duck carcass and any reserved bones
- Duck meat scraps
- 1 onion, halved
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 inch piece fresh ginger, sliced
- 2 star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 2 tbsp Shaoxing rice wine
- 1 tbsp Chinese red vinegar (Zhenjiang/Chinkiang)
- Cold water to cover
- Salt to taste

### Instructions
1. Roast the duck carcass and bones at 425°F for 20 to 25 minutes until deeply browned.
2. Transfer to a large stockpot. Add onion, garlic, ginger, star anise, cinnamon, Shaoxing wine, and red vinegar. Cover with cold water.
3. Bring to a boil, skim off any foam, then reduce to a low simmer.
4. Simmer uncovered for 3 to 4 hours until the stock is deeply flavored and has reduced by about a third.
5. Strain through a fine mesh sieve. Season lightly with salt. Set aside.

---

## Caramelized Onion Soup

### Ingredients
- 4 yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 4 red onions, thinly sliced
- ½ cup rendered Peking duck fat (substitute unsalted butter if needed)
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- 5 cups duck stock
- 3 tbsp Shaoxing rice wine, divided
- 1 tbsp Chinese red vinegar
- ¼ tsp five spice powder, plus more to taste during cooking
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 2 bay leaves
- Salt, black and white pepper, to taste

### Optional Additions
- 1 to 2 packets unflavored gelatin, for added body
- 2 tbsp duck and/or veal demi-glace, stirred in with the stock, for deeper umami

---

## Instructions

**Slice the onions**
1. Thinly slice all 8 onions. To reduce the crying response, place the onions in the freezer for 15 to 20 minutes before slicing.

**Caramelize the onions**
2. Heat duck fat and olive oil together in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-low heat.
3. Add sliced onions and stir to coat. Cook low and slow, stirring occasionally, for at least 1 hour until deeply golden and almost black at the edges.

**Deglaze**
4. Add 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine and the red vinegar. Stir to deglaze, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot. Cook for 2 minutes until the liquid absorbs.

**Build the soup**
5. Add duck stock, five spice powder, thyme, and bay leaves. Add demi-glace here if using. Season with salt and white pepper.
6. Bring to a rolling boil, then reduce to a low simmer.

**Bloom and add gelatin (if using)**
7. Pour 2 tbsp cold water into a small bowl. Sprinkle the gelatin evenly over the surface and let it sit undisturbed for 5 minutes until it blooms into a thick, opaque gel. Do not stir during this time. In the final 5 minutes of simmering, spoon a ladleful of hot soup into the bloomed gelatin and whisk to dissolve, then stir the mixture back into the pot.

**Simmer and refresh**
8. Simmer uncovered for at least 1 to 2 hours. You can simmer for 4 to 12 hours, letting it reduce and concentrate, then thin back out with additional stock or water as needed.
9. Every 1 to 2 hours, taste the soup and consider refreshing: add a small splash of Shaoxing wine and a pinch of five spice to keep both aromas present and forward as the soup cooks down. These volatile compounds fade with prolonged heat, so periodic small additions maintain their character without overpowering.
10. Remove bay leaves. Just before serving, stir in the remaining 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine off the heat.
11. Taste and adjust seasoning. Add a splash more red vinegar if the soup needs brightness.

**Toast the bread**
12. Butter or brush the bread slices with duck fat and toast in a pan or under the broiler until very firm and golden on both sides.

**Broil and serve**
13. Preheat the broiler.
14. Ladle hot soup into oven-safe bowls. Float a slice of toasted bread on top of each.
15. Cover generously with grated Gruyère.
16. Place under the broiler for 2 to 4 minutes until the cheese is bubbling and deeply golden. Watch closely.
17. Serve immediately.

---

## Optional Substitutions

**Gluten-Free**
- Substitute GF bread for the crusty bread. Double toast until very firm so it holds up under the cheese and broiler without going soggy.
- Substitute pale dry sherry for the Shaoxing wine in equal quantities at the same stages.

## Notes

**The Four Levers**
This soup has four variables you can adjust throughout the cook to shape its final character:

- **Shaoxing wine:** Adds aromatic lift and fermented depth. Volatile compounds fade with heat, so add small splashes periodically and a final pour off the heat before serving. More Shaoxing pushes the soup toward brightness and complexity.
- **Five spice:** Adds warmth and a subtle Chinese backbone. Like Shaoxing, it fades over a long cook. Refresh with small pinches every hour or two. More five spice deepens the Chinese character of the dish; less keeps it closer to a classic French onion.
- **Fat (duck fat, butter, demi-glace):** Fat carries flavor and adds body and richness. Duck fat ties everything to the bird. Butter softens and rounds. Demi-glace adds concentrated umami and a silkier texture. Add any of these at any point during the cook to push the soup in a richer direction.
- **Red vinegar:** Adds brightness and cuts through richness. Use it to balance the soup if it becomes too heavy or flat. A small splash at the end lifts everything.

**General Notes**
- Caramelize the onions for at least one hour, until almost black at the edges. Pale onions produce a flat soup.
- The longer the soup simmers, the better. Two hours is the minimum. Four to twelve hours is ideal.
- Simmering uncovered concentrates the flavor. When it gets too thick, add water or stock and keep going.
- Rendered Peking duck fat is the key building block. Save every drop from the roasting pan.
- Chinese red vinegar adds depth and slight sweetness rather than sharp acidity. Do not substitute with rice vinegar.
- Duck fat on the bread ties it directly into the soup and is worth doing if you have it.
- The stock can be made a day ahead. Refrigerate overnight and skim the solidified fat from the surface before reheating. Save it alongside the rendered roasting fat.
