## Ingredients

- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 4 pounds oxtail or beef shoulder, cut into 1- or 2-inch pieces
- Salt
- 8 medium onions
- 4 celery stalks, coarsely chopped
- 4 medium carrots, peeled and coarsely chopped
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 thyme sprigs
- 8 tablespoons (1 stick) unsalted butter
- Black pepper
- 1 cup port wine
- Lemon juice, to taste, optional
- 6 ounces baguette loaf, cut into 1/2-inch-thick slices
- 2 garlic cloves, halved
- 8 ounces Gruyère cheese

## Instructions

1. Heat the oil in a 6-quart Dutch oven over high heat. Sear the beef in a single layer, in batches if needed, until browned underneath (do not turn). Salt generously and set aside.
2. Coarsely chop 2 onions and add to the pot with the celery, carrots, bay leaves, and thyme. Cook over medium about 10 minutes, stirring, until soft and starting to caramelize. Return the beef, add 8 cups water, and simmer gently 2 1/2 to 3 hours, until the meat is very tender.
3. Remove the beef and save it for another use. Strain the broth through a fine sieve, pressing on the solids; discard them. Add water if needed to make 10 cups.
4. Halve and thinly slice the remaining 6 onions. Melt the butter in the pot over medium heat and cook the onions 45 minutes to 1 hour, tossing occasionally, until deep golden. Season with 1 teaspoon salt and pepper.
5. Add the port and cook 3 minutes, scraping the bottom. Add the broth and simmer over low 30 minutes. Season with salt and lemon juice if desired.
6. Meanwhile, toast the bread at 350°F about 12 minutes until golden, then rub with the cut garlic.
7. Heat the broiler with a rack 4 to 6 inches from the heat. Thinly slice 3 ounces of the Gruyère and grate the rest. Float the toasts on the soup, cover with the slices, and scatter the grated cheese on top. Broil the whole pot 3 to 5 minutes, watching closely, until bubbling and golden.
8. Cut the bread and cheese into portions with scissors and ladle into bowls.

## Notes

- Part of the soup can be refrigerated and reheated later for a smaller group.
- The braised beef is not served in the soup; the source sets it aside for another use.
