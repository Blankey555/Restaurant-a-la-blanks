## Components

- Herb Panisse
- Mustard Velouté
- Tarragon Pesto
- Castelfranco
- Pan-Seared Salmon

---

## Herb Panisse

### Ingredients
- 2 cups water
- 1 cup chickpea flour (besan)
- 1 tbsp olive oil, plus more for frying
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp white pepper
- 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves
- 1 tbsp fresh chives, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped

### Instructions
1. Lightly oil a small baking dish or loaf pan and set aside.
2. Bring the water to a simmer in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Whisk in the chickpea flour gradually to avoid lumps.
3. Add olive oil, salt, and white pepper. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon, for 10 to 15 minutes until the mixture thickens and pulls away from the sides of the pan.
4. Stir in the herbs. Pour into the prepared dish and smooth the surface. Refrigerate uncovered for at least 1 hour until fully set and firm.
5. Once set, turn out onto a cutting board and slice into rectangles or rounds approximately ½ inch thick.
6. Heat a generous amount of olive oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high heat. Fry the panisse pieces for 3 to 4 minutes per side until deeply golden and crisp. Drain briefly on paper towels. Season with flaky salt.

---

## Mustard Velouté

### Ingredients
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 small shallot, finely minced
- ½ cup dry white wine
- 1 cup good quality chicken or vegetable stock
- ½ cup heavy cream
- 1½ tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp wholegrain mustard
- Salt and white pepper, to taste
- Squeeze of lemon juice

### Instructions
1. Melt butter in a small saucepan over medium heat. Add shallot and sweat until soft and translucent, about 3 minutes.
2. Add white wine and reduce by half.
3. Add stock and reduce by half again.
4. Add cream and simmer gently until the sauce coats the back of a spoon, about 5 minutes.
5. Remove from heat and whisk in both mustards. Season with salt, white pepper, and lemon juice.
6. Keep warm over very low heat. Do not boil after adding the mustard.

---

## Tarragon Pesto

### Ingredients
- 1 cup fresh tarragon leaves, loosely packed
- ½ cup fresh flat-leaf parsley leaves
- 1 small clove garlic
- 2 tbsp toasted pine nuts or blanched almonds
- ¼ cup good quality olive oil
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- Salt and pepper, to taste

### Instructions
1. Combine tarragon, parsley, garlic, and nuts in a food processor. Pulse until roughly chopped.
2. With the motor running, drizzle in the olive oil until a loose, bright pesto forms.
3. Add lemon juice, salt, and pepper. Adjust consistency with a little more oil if needed.
4. Set aside at room temperature. The pesto will hold for several hours without oxidizing significantly due to the parsley.

---

## Castelfranco

### Ingredients
- 1 small head castelfranco radicchio, leaves separated and torn
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 small shallot, thinly sliced
- Splash of white wine or white wine vinegar
- Salt and pepper, to taste

### Instructions
1. Melt butter in a wide pan over medium heat. Add shallot and cook until softened, about 2 minutes.
2. Add the castelfranco and toss briefly in the pan. Add the splash of wine or vinegar and wilt just until the leaves soften slightly but retain some texture, about 1 minute.
3. Season with salt and pepper. Remove from heat and keep warm.

---

## Pan-Seared Salmon

### Ingredients
- 2 skin-on salmon fillets (approximately 6 oz / 175g each)
- Salt and white pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 sprig thyme
- 1 clove garlic, crushed

### Instructions
1. Pat salmon completely dry. Season the flesh side with salt and white pepper. Leave skin unseasoned until just before cooking.
2. Heat oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high heat until just beginning to smoke.
3. Season the skin side and place salmon skin side down. Press gently with a spatula for the first 30 seconds to prevent curling.
4. Cook skin side down for 4 to 5 minutes until the skin is deeply crisp and the cook line has risen to about two-thirds of the fillet.
5. Add butter, thyme, and garlic to the pan. Baste the flesh side with the foaming butter.
6. Flip and cook for 1 minute on the flesh side. Remove from heat and rest for 2 minutes.

---

## To Plate

1. Spoon a pool of mustard velouté onto each warm plate.
2. Place a piece or two of panisse slightly off-center.
3. Arrange a small nest of wilted castelfranco alongside.
4. Set the salmon skin side up on top of or beside the panisse.
5. Spoon tarragon pesto over and around the plate.
6. Finish with a few drops of good olive oil and flaky salt if desired.

---

## Notes
- The panisse and both sauces can be made several hours ahead. Fry the panisse to order.
- Castelfranco is milder and more delicate than other radicchios. Do not overcook. It should retain some bitterness and structure.
- The velouté should be loose enough to pool on the plate. If it thickens too much, loosen with a splash of warm stock.
- Tarragon is assertive. Taste the pesto as you go and adjust the parsley ratio if needed to balance the anise flavor.
