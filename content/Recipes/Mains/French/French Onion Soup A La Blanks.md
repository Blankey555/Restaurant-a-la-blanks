## Ingredients

### Soup
- 4 yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 4 red onions, thinly sliced
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- 5 cups beef stock (see optional scratch recipe below)
- 2 tbsp brandy or cognac (dry sherry works as a substitute)
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 2 bay leaves
- Black pepper, to taste
- Salt, to taste

### Optional Addition
- 1 to 2 short ribs

### Bread and Cheese
- 4 thick slices crusty bread (sourdough or baguette)
- Unsalted butter, for toasting
- 1½ cups Gruyère, grated

---

## Optional: Short Rib Addition

If adding short rib, start here before caramelizing the onions.

1. Season short ribs generously with salt and black pepper.
2. Heat a splash of oil in the pot over high heat. Sear short ribs on all sides until deeply browned, about 3 to 4 minutes per side. Remove and set aside.
3. Pour off excess fat, leaving about 2 tbsp in the pot. Use this fat in place of or in addition to the butter and olive oil for frying the onions.
4. Proceed with caramelizing the onions as directed below.
5. Once the soup is simmering, return the short ribs to the pot. Remove the bones before serving and shred any remaining meat back into the soup.

---

## Instructions

**Slice the onions**

1. Thinly slice all 8 onions. To reduce the crying response, place the onions in the freezer for 15 to 20 minutes before slicing. A sharp knife also helps significantly.

**Caramelize the onions**

2. Melt butter and olive oil together in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-low heat.
3. Add the sliced onions and stir to coat. Cook low and slow, stirring occasionally, for at least 1 hour until deeply golden and almost black at the edges. The darker they get, the sweeter and more complex the soup will be.

**Deglaze**

4. Add the brandy or cognac and stir to deglaze, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot. Cook for 2 minutes until the alcohol absorbs.

**Build the soup**

5. Add the beef stock, thyme, and bay leaves. Season with salt and black pepper.
6. Bring to a rolling boil, then reduce to a low simmer.
7. Simmer uncovered for at least 1 to 2 hours. You can simmer for 4 to 12 hours, letting it reduce and concentrate, then thin back out with additional stock or water as needed.
8. Remove bay leaves. Taste and adjust seasoning before serving.

**Toast the bread**

9. Butter the bread slices generously and toast in a pan or under the broiler until very firm and golden on both sides.

**Broil and serve**

10. Preheat the broiler.
11. Ladle hot soup into oven-safe bowls. Float a slice of toasted bread on top of each.
12. Cover generously with grated Gruyère.
13. Place under the broiler for 2 to 4 minutes until the cheese is bubbling and deeply golden. Watch closely.
14. Serve immediately.

## Optional: Homemade Beef Stock from Scratch

### Ingredients
- 3 to 4 lb beef bones (marrow bones, knuckles, or oxtail)
- 1 onion, halved
- 3 carrots, roughly chopped
- 3 stalks celery, roughly chopped
- 6 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tsp black peppercorns
- Fresh thyme sprigs
- Cold water to cover

### Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 425°F. Arrange bones on a roasting pan and roast for 30 to 40 minutes until deeply browned, turning once.
2. Transfer bones to a large stockpot. Add all remaining ingredients. Cover with cold water.
3. Bring to a boil and skim off any foam. Reduce to a very low simmer.
4. Simmer uncovered for 6 to 12 hours, adding water as needed to keep bones submerged.
5. Strain through a fine mesh sieve. Season lightly with salt.
6. Refrigerate overnight. Skim the solidified fat from the surface before using.

---

## Notes
- Caramelize the onions for at least one hour, until almost black at the edges. Pale onions produce a flat soup.
- The longer the soup simmers, the better. Two hours is the minimum. Four to twelve hours is ideal.
- Simmering uncovered concentrates the flavor. When it gets too thick, add water or stock and keep going.
- Brandy or cognac adds more depth than sherry. Add it before the stock so the alcohol cooks off.
- Gruyère melts cleanly, browns well under the broiler, and has the sharpness the soup needs.
- If using short ribs, the collagen released during the long braise gives the soup a silky body that stock alone cannot replicate.
