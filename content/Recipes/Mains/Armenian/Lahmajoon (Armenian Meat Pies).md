## Ingredients

### Dough
- 1 package dry yeast
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 1 cup lukewarm water
- 3 1/2 cups flour, divided: 3 cups for mixing, remaining 1/2 cup for kneading and rolling as needed
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 3 tablespoons melted shortening
- oil

### Meat Filling
- 1 pound total ground lamb and beef, blended in your preferred ratio
- 2 medium onions, chopped fine
- 1/2 cup minced parsley
- 1 red pepper, chopped fine
- 1/2 green pepper, chopped fine
- 1 can (16 oz) Italian-style tomatoes, drained
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 2 tablespoons flour
- 1 teaspoon crushed dried mint
- 1 clove garlic, mashed
- 2 teaspoons salt
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper
- Lemon wedges, for serving

## Instructions

1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in one-quarter cup lukewarm water. Stir and let stand a few minutes to proof.
2. In a large bowl combine three cups flour and the salt. Make a well in the center and add the yeast mixture, melted shortening and remaining water. Mix.
3. Knead until dough is smooth and elastic, about five minutes. Add flour from the reserved 1/2 cup only as needed; keep any remainder for rolling. Lightly oil surface of dough. Place a sheet of plastic wrap across bowl, wrap well, and place in a warm, draft-free place until doubled in bulk, or about three hours.
4. Mix together all the ingredients for filling. Remove tomatoes from can and crush them before adding to mixture. Do not use the juice remaining in can. Cook a small sample of the filling to 160°F (71°C), then taste it and adjust the seasoning. Do not taste the raw mixture. Cover and refrigerate the remaining filling until ready to assemble.
5. Punch down dough when doubled in bulk and shape into fourteen balls. Place them on a tray and cover with a plastic sheet or dish towel. Set aside for ten minutes.
6. Roll each ball into a circle about six inches in diameter, using the reserved flour on the surface and rolling pin as needed. Place circles on greased cookie tray (or line tray with baking parchment, ungreased). Spread two or three tablespoonfuls of meat mixture over the entire surface of circle.
7. Bake in preheated 450°F oven. Place tray on lower rack for six minutes then on upper rack for six minutes. The bottoms should be lightly browned. Check the meat topping reaches 160°F (71°C) with a thin-tip thermometer; sizzling and color alone do not confirm doneness. Timing varies with topping thickness and the oven. Serve hot with lemon wedges. Squeeze lemon over just before eating.

## Notes

- Meat pies may be baked and frozen. Freeze with meat sides faced together and pack in a plastic bag, secured tightly with a twister seal. Suggested reheating method (not kitchen-tested for this recipe): thaw overnight in the refrigerator, separate the pies, and arrange meat-side up on a baking sheet. Heat in a preheated 350°F (175°C) oven until the filling reaches 165°F (74°C). Cover loosely with foil if the bread browns before the center is hot; do not rely on a fixed five-minute time. Refrigerate cooked pies within 2 hours and freeze promptly once chilled.
- Variation 1: divide dough into three balls, divide meat mixture in three sections, roll each ball out to fit a cookie tray. Use the same oven temperature and rack sequence as a starting point, checking the meat reaches 160°F (71°C); larger sheets may need a different baking time. Cut into squares and serve.
- Variation 2: use English muffins instead of dough. Split 12 or 14 muffins in half, toast tops and spread with meat mixture. Use 450°F as a starting point and check the meat reaches 160°F (71°C); muffin thickness changes the baking time. Cover loosely with foil if the bread browns too quickly.
- Variation 3: use Armenian lavash bread instead of the dough. Brush baking pan with melted butter, place lavash in pan, spread with meat mixture and bake at 450°F until the meat reaches 160°F (71°C), checking early because thin lavash can brown quickly. The saved fifteen-minute estimate is only a guide.
- Variation 4: for an interesting taste, sprinkle unbaked tops of pies with pomegranate seeds.
