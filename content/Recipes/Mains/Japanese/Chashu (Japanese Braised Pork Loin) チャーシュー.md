## Ingredients

- 1 boneless pork loin, 2 to 2½ lb (907 to 1134g)
- 1 Tokyo negi, green part only (or 1 leek, or 2 to 3 green onions)
- 1 inch fresh ginger, unpeeled, cut into 4 slices
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 cup sake
- 1 cup soy sauce
- 2 cups water
- ⅔ cup sugar
- 2 tbsp mirin (added to original recipe; helps compensate for the lack of fat)

---

## Key Differences from Pork Belly Version

- **Shorter braise time:** 45 to 60 minutes instead of 2 hours. Loin has no fat to render and will turn dry and chalky if overcooked.
- **Target internal temp:** Pull at 145°F (63°C). Do not push higher.
- **Rolling is easier:** Loin is already a compact cylinder. Roll and tie as normal or skip rolling entirely since the shape holds itself.
- **More forgiving on the rest:** The overnight marinade is even more important here since the loin needs the braising liquid to compensate for its lack of fat.

---

## Instructions

### Day 1

**Roll and tie**

1. If the loin is irregular in shape, roll and tie with butcher twine at ½-inch intervals to keep it uniform. If it's already a compact cylinder, tying is optional but helps it hold its shape when sliced.

**Sear**

2. Heat oil in a cast iron skillet over high heat. Sear all sides until evenly golden brown, about 8 to 10 minutes total. Don't skip this step. It adds flavor the lean meat needs.

**Braise**

3. Combine sake, soy sauce, water, sugar, and mirin in a heavy-bottomed pot just large enough to hold the loin. Add negi and ginger.
4. Transfer the seared loin to the pot. Bring to a boil over medium heat.
5. Skim off any foam and scum. Reduce to low heat.
6. Place an otoshibuta (drop lid) directly on the meat. Simmer on low for 45 to 60 minutes, turning every 15 minutes. Check internal temperature at 45 minutes. Pull at 145°F (63°C).
7. Turn off heat and let cool slightly in the liquid.

**Marinate overnight**

8. Transfer the pork to a container or vacuum bag with 1 cup of the strained braising liquid (more than the belly version; loin needs extra liquid contact to stay moist). Seal and refrigerate for at least 8 hours.

### Day 2

9. Remove from the bag and cut off twine if used.
10. Slice crosswise into ¼-inch rounds.
11. Optional: sear briefly with a kitchen torch. Keep it quick. Loin dries out faster than belly under heat.
12. Drizzle generously with warmed braising sauce before serving to add back moisture and richness.

---

## Storage
- **Fridge:** Up to 5 days (shorter than belly due to lower fat content)
- **Freezer:** Up to 1 month. Defrost overnight in the fridge.
- **Braising sauce:** Up to 1 month in the fridge.

---

## Notes
- Do not cook past 145°F. Loin has no fat to keep it moist and overcooks quickly.
- The mirin addition helps replicate some of the sweetness and gloss that belly fat naturally provides.
- Serve with extra braising sauce drizzled on top. This is more important with loin than belly.
- Scraps and ends work well in fried rice or chopped into ramen.
