## Ingredients

### Rolled Chashu Log (serves 8 to 10)
- 1 block pork belly, 2 to 2½ lb (907 to 1134g), rind removed
- 1 Tokyo negi, green part only (or 1 leek, or 2 to 3 green onions)
- 1 inch fresh ginger, unpeeled, cut into 4 slices
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 cup sake
- 1 cup soy sauce
- 2 cups water
- ⅔ cup sugar

### Non-Rolled Chashu Block (serves 3)
- ¾ lb pork belly block
- 1 Tokyo negi, green part only (or 1 leek, or 2 to 3 green onions)
- 1 inch fresh ginger, unpeeled, sliced thin
- ½ tbsp neutral oil
- ⅓ cup sake
- ⅓ cup soy sauce
- ⅔ cup water
- 3 tbsp sugar

### For 1 lb Non-Rolled Block (serves 4)
- 1 lb pork belly block (454g)
- ½ tbsp neutral oil
- ½ cup sake
- ½ cup soy sauce
- 1 cup water
- ⅓ cup sugar (5 tbsp)

---

## Instructions

### Method 1: Rolled Chashu Log

#### Day 1

**Roll and tie the pork belly**

1. Orient the slab so the layered meat-and-fat pattern is visible at one or both ends.
2. Roll into a compact log and trim any excess.
3. Run butcher twine under the far end and tie a double knot, leaving a 3-inch tail.
4. Wrap tightly around that end 2 to 3 times, then pull the twine to the opposite end and wrap 2 to 3 times there.
5. Work back toward the start, wrapping every ⅓ inch (1 cm), as tightly as possible.
6. Tuck the twine under several middle wraps, reverse direction, and run it back under the wraps to the original knot.
7. Tie a double knot with the tail and cut the excess.

**Sear**

8. Heat oil in a cast iron skillet over high heat. Add the tied pork belly.
9. Sear all sides until evenly golden brown, about 10 to 15 minutes total.

**Braise**

10. Combine sake, soy sauce, water, and sugar in a heavy-bottomed pot just large enough to hold the roll. Add negi and ginger.
11. Transfer the seared pork belly to the pot. Bring to a boil over medium heat.
12. Skim off any foam and scum. Reduce to low heat.
13. Place an otoshibuta (drop lid) directly on the meat. Simmer on low for 2 hours, turning the roll every 30 minutes. Keep the drop lid on at all times.
14. Turn off heat and let cool slightly.

**Marinate overnight**

15. Transfer the pork to a container or vacuum bag with ½ cup of the strained braising liquid. Seal and refrigerate for at least 8 hours. Optionally add peeled soft-boiled eggs to the remaining liquid to make ramen eggs.

#### Day 2

16. Remove the pork from the bag and cut off all twine with kitchen shears.
17. Slice crosswise into ¼-inch rounds.
18. Optional: sear the surface with a kitchen torch until lightly charred (aburi chashu).
19. Skim and strain the remaining braising sauce. Store in a jar in the fridge for up to 1 month. Use for drizzling, stir-fries, marinades, or ramen eggs.

---

### Method 2: Non-Rolled Chashu Block

1. Heat oil in a cast iron skillet over high heat. Sear the pork belly block fat-side down first, then flip. About 10 minutes total.
2. While searing, combine negi, ginger, sake, soy sauce, water, and sugar in a pot just large enough to hold the pork.
3. Add the seared pork to the pot. Bring to a boil, skimming foam. Reduce to low.
4. Place an otoshibuta on the meat. Simmer on low for 1 hour, turning every 15 minutes.
5. After 1 hour, choose:
   - **Serve immediately:** Remove drop lid and reduce sauce further until thickened.
   - **Recommended:** Transfer pork and a little sauce to a container. Refrigerate overnight.
6. Slice into ¼-inch pieces. Sear with a torch or reheat by soaking in hot braising sauce.

---

## Storage
- **Fridge:** Up to 7 days
- **Freezer:** Up to 1 month. Defrost overnight in the fridge before using.
- **Braising sauce:** Keeps up to 1 month in the fridge. Use for ramen eggs, marinades, and stir-fries.

---

## Notes
- Don't skip the overnight rest. It's what separates good chashu from great chashu.
- Pork belly is not bacon. Use a skinless block from a butcher or Korean grocery store.
- Pork shoulder works as a substitute but won't have the same silky texture.
- Leftover edges and scraps are ideal for chashu fried rice.
- Ramen eggs: add peeled soft-boiled eggs to the braising liquid alongside the pork and marinate overnight.
