*Thinly sliced marinated beef with a sweet, savory, slightly spicy marinade. Cooks in minutes.*

**Prep:** 15 minutes (plus 2 hours to overnight marinating)
**Cook:** 15 minutes
**Servings:** 6

---

## Ingredients

- 1½ lb boneless ribeye steak (or top sirloin, flank, or skirt steak)
- ½ small pear, peeled and coarsely grated (or Fuji apple)
- ¼ cup reduced sodium soy sauce
- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 1 tbsp gochujang (Korean red chili paste)
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil, divided
- 2 green onions, thinly sliced (garnish)
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (garnish)

---

## Instructions

**Slice the beef**
1. Wrap the steak in plastic wrap and freeze for 30 minutes. This firms it up for easier thin slicing.
2. Unwrap and slice across the grain into ¼-inch thick pieces.

**Marinate**
3. In a bowl, combine pear, soy sauce, brown sugar, sesame oil, garlic, ginger, and gochujang.
4. Add the sliced beef to a gallon zip-lock bag and pour in the marinade. Seal and turn to coat.
5. Marinate for at least 2 hours, up to overnight in the fridge. Turn the bag occasionally.

**Cook**
6. Heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil in a cast iron grill pan or skillet over medium-high heat.
7. Working in batches, add beef in a single layer. Cook 2 to 3 minutes per side until charred and cooked through. Do not overcrowd the pan or the beef will steam instead of sear.
8. Repeat with remaining oil and beef.

**Serve**
9. Garnish with green onions and sesame seeds. Serve immediately over steamed rice with kimchi, cucumbers, perilla leaves, and ssamjang on the side.

---

## Storage
- **Fridge:** Up to 3 days in an airtight container.
- **Freeze before cooking:** Transfer marinated beef to a freezer bag. Freeze up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge before cooking.
- **Freeze after cooking:** Cool completely, freeze up to 2 months. Thaw overnight, reheat over medium-high heat.

---

## Notes
- Pre-sliced bulgogi beef is available at Korean and Japanese grocery stores and saves time.
- Cook in small batches. An overcrowded pan steams the meat and kills the caramelization.
- The pear tenderizes the meat as well as sweetening the marinade. Don't skip it.
- Reduced sodium soy sauce is recommended. Regular soy sauce makes the marinade very salty.
- A cast iron pan gives the best sear and char. A regular skillet works but won't replicate the caramelized edges as well.
