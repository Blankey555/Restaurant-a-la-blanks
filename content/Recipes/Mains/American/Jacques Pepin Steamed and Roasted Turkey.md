## Ingredients

- 1 16- to 18-pound turkey, preferably fresh and organic, with neck, gizzard, heart and liver; knobby ends of drumsticks cut off so the sinews can be removed later (a butcher can do this)
- 1 1/2 cups diced (1/2-inch) carrots
- 2 cups diced (1/2-inch) onions
- 1/2 cup apple cider
- 2 tablespoons cider vinegar
- 1 teaspoon Tabasco or other hot pepper sauce
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 cup white wine
- 2 teaspoons potato starch or cornstarch dissolved in 2 tablespoons water
- Salt and black pepper

## Instructions

1. Turkey: trim the drumstick ends if not done. Cut about 1 inch deep and 1 1/2 inches long at the drumstick-thigh joint of each leg and at each wing-breast joint.
2. Set a small rack (or crumpled foil) in a large stockpot with 6 cups water. Add the turkey (without neck and giblets), bring to a boil, cover, reduce to low and steam about 30 minutes.
3. Glaze: mix the cider, vinegar, Tabasco and salt.
4. Take the pot off the heat and heat the oven to 375°F. Move the turkey breast side up to a roasting pan, reserving the steaming stock. Roast 30 minutes, then scatter the carrots and onions around and toss in the pan fat. Brush with glaze and roast 1 1/2 hours more, glazing occasionally; tent with foil if the top darkens too much.
5. Meanwhile, let the stock settle about 10 minutes and skim off the fat. Simmer it, partly covered, with the neck, gizzard and heart until reduced to 4 cups, about 1 hour. Pick the neck meat, chop it with the gizzard and heart (about 2 cups) and return to the stock.
6. Remove the turkey when breast and leg reach about 160°F. Move to an ovenproof platter and pull the drumstick sinews out through the tines of a fork with small pliers. Keep warm, uncovered, in a 160°F oven.
7. Sauce: add the stock and giblets to the roasting pan vegetables and scrape up the bottom. Transfer to a saucepan, rest 5 minutes and skim the fat. Over medium-low heat add the wine and starch slurry, bring to a boil, stirring, until thickened. Simmer 1 minute, season and serve in a warm sauceboat.

## Notes

- The source suggests sautéing the liver in butter with salt and pepper as a cook's snack.
