## Ingredients

- 1 whole beef brisket, both point and flat cuts, 10 to 14 pounds
- 2/3 cup black peppercorns
- 1/2 to 2/3 cup coarse salt, preferably Morton's kosher salt
- Hardwood charcoal
- 3 cups wood chips, preferably oak, soaked at least 1 hour or overnight

## Instructions

1. Unwrap the brisket and pat dry. With the fat side up (thicker point on top), trim the fat cap to an even 1/2 inch.
2. Grind the peppercorns coarsely and sift out the fine dust, leaving about 1/2 cup. Mix with an equal amount of salt and pack it all over the brisket, making more mix if needed. Cook right away, or set on a rack in a sheet pan, cover loosely and refrigerate 8 to 36 hours for a crustier bark.
3. Clean the grates of a charcoal grill (at least 22 inches if a kettle) or smoker. Light a half chimney of charcoal, let it ash over, dump it on one side and close the lid. When the temperature holds at 225 to 250°F, add half the wood chips over the coals, replace the grate, and set the brisket on it fat side up. Cover.
4. Cook at about 225°F, adding charcoal and chips to hold temperature, 4 to 6 hours, until the thickest part reads 170 to 180°F.
5. Wrap in unwaxed parchment or butcher paper, then foil. Continue on the grill or in a 225°F oven 2 to 4 hours more, testing from 1 1/2 hours, until the meat feels soft and almost jiggly.
6. Rest at least 30 minutes. Unwrap, separate the point from the flat, trim remaining fat and membrane, and slice 1/2 inch thick.
7. Serve with white bread, or pinto beans, potato salad and coleslaw; or pile into buttered toasted soft rolls with raw white onion and pickled cucumber and jalapeño.

## Notes

- Wrapped well and held in a cooler, the cooked brisket stays hot for at least 4 hours (per the source).
