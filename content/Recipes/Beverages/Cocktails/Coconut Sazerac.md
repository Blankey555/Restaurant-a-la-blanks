*A cognac-forward Sazerac with a toasted coconut garnish. The absinthe rinse and Peychaud's keep it classical while the coconut adds a subtle tropical note on the nose.*

---

## Ingredients

- 1½ oz Pierre Ferrand cognac
- ½ oz Wild Turkey rye
- 3 dashes Peychaud's bitters
- 1 barspoon simple syrup (or 1 small sugar cube)
- Pernod Absinthe, for rinsing
- Toasted coconut flakes, for garnish
- Lemon peel, for expressing (optional but recommended)

---

## Instructions

1. Place a rocks glass in the freezer to chill. Alternatively, fill with ice and let sit while you build the drink.
2. In a mixing glass, combine cognac, rye, Peychaud's bitters, and simple syrup. Add ice and stir for 30 seconds until well chilled and diluted.
3. Remove the rocks glass from the freezer or discard the ice. Add a small pour of absinthe and swirl to coat the entire interior of the glass. Discard the excess.
4. Strain the stirred cocktail into the absinthe-rinsed glass over no ice, or one large rock if preferred.
5. Express a lemon peel over the surface and discard or place on the rim.
6. Garnish with toasted coconut flakes on the surface or rim of the glass.

---

## Notes
- The absinthe rinse should be light. Too much will overpower the cognac.
- Stir, do not shake. Shaking introduces unwanted dilution and aeration in a spirit-forward drink.
- Toast coconut flakes in a dry pan over medium heat, stirring constantly, until golden and fragrant. Watch closely — they burn fast.
- A frosty glass is traditional for a Sazerac. The freezer method is preferred over ice since it keeps the drink undiluted.
- Pierre Ferrand Ambre or 1840 both work well here. The 1840 will give a drier, more assertive result.
