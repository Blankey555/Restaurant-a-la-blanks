## Ingredients

- 2 cups plus 3 tablespoons sugar
- 1 1/2 cups water
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice
- 2 cups finely chopped walnuts
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 pound phyllo dough
- 1 1/2 cups clarified butter, melted
- 1 dowel, 3/4 x 15 inches

## Instructions

1. Boil together two cups sugar and water for ten minutes and add lemon juice the last few minutes. Shut flame and set syrup aside to cool.
2. Combine walnuts, cinnamon and three tablespoons sugar in a small bowl. Set nut filling aside.
3. Remove the dough from its plastic wrapper, unfold and cover it with a slightly dampened towel to prevent the sheets from drying. Work quickly with one sheet at a time and keep remaining sheets always covered.
4. Place sheet with the long end nearest you. Brush surface with melted butter and fold the right half over the left half. Brush surface again with butter. Sprinkle a tablespoonful of the filling over the lower half. Place dowel at bottom and roll up loosely to the end.
5. With fingers at both ends of the pastry, gently squeeze towards center to give it a crimped, pleated effect. Carefully push pastry off dowel and place on buttered baking sheet, seam side down. Roll will be about four to five inches long. Continue with remaining sheets until pan is full. Brush tops with melted butter.
6. Bake in preheated 350 degree oven twenty minutes until they are a golden color, top and bottom.
7. Dip warm rolls in the cooled syrup. If smaller pastries are desired, cut rolls in half before syruping.

**Yield:** About 30 long pastries.

## Notes

- Boorma may be baked in advance but do not syrup. On the day they are to be served, heat rolls in a 350 degree oven for ten minutes, then dip them in the cooled syrup.
- A one pound package of phyllo contains 25 to 35 sheets depending on the brand.
- Variation, Bird's Nest Pastries: add half a cup chopped pistachios to the walnut mixture and set aside one third of the nut mixture. Use a thinner dowel and cut phyllo sheets in half. Do not fold the pastry and use a teaspoon of filling. Roll pastry to within one inch of the end, then place on the baking sheet in a circle shape so the protruding strip forms the bottom of the nest. Bake as directed. After baking, fill the nest hollows with a spoonful of the remaining chopped nuts and spoon the cooled syrup over the warm nests.
