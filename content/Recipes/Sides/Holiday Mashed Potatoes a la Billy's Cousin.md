# Holiday Mashed Potatoes

*Unapologetically rich mashed potatoes built on herb-simmered cream and butter. Optional Greek yogurt for tang, optional cheese for indulgence.*

**Serves:** 8
**Active Time:** 20 minutes
**Total Time:** 1 hour

---

## Ingredients

### Potatoes
- 4 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cubed (russets work for a fluffier texture)
- Salted water for boiling

### Herb-Simmered Cream and Butter
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 1 stick (8 tbsp) unsalted butter, cubed, plus more for finishing
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 shallots, halved
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- Small bunch fresh chives, left whole
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp white pepper

### Optional
- ½ cup Greek yogurt, for tang
- 1 cup Gruyère, sharp cheddar, or Parmesan, grated, for cheese mashed potatoes
- Fresh chives, finely sliced, for garnish
- Flaky salt, for finishing

---

## Instructions

**Simmer the cream and butter**

1. Combine cream, butter, garlic, shallots, thyme, rosemary, and chives in a small saucepan over low heat.
2. Heat gently until the butter is fully melted and the mixture just begins to steam. Do not boil.
3. Simmer on the lowest possible heat for 20 to 30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the cream is fragrant and infused with the aromatics.
4. Strain through a fine mesh sieve, pressing the solids to extract as much flavor as possible. Discard the solids. Keep warm.

**Cook the potatoes**

5. Place cubed potatoes in a large pot and cover with cold salted water.
6. Bring to a boil and cook until completely tender and a fork meets no resistance, about 20 minutes.
7. Drain thoroughly. Return the potatoes to the pot and place over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, shaking occasionally, to drive off excess moisture.

**Mash**

8. Pass the potatoes through a ricer or food mill for the smoothest result. Alternatively, use a masher for a more rustic texture. Do not use a food processor or blender: it will make the potatoes gluey.
9. Add the warm herb-infused cream and butter gradually, folding it in with a spatula or wooden spoon until fully incorporated and smooth. Use as much as the potatoes will absorb.
10. If using Greek yogurt, fold it in now. Taste before adjusting salt. The yogurt adds tang and slight acidity that brightens the richness.
11. If making cheese mashed potatoes, fold in the grated cheese while the potatoes are still hot and stir until fully melted and incorporated.
12. Taste and adjust salt and white pepper.

**Finish and serve**

13. Transfer to a warm serving bowl. Make a well in the center and add a generous knob of butter to melt on top.
14. Garnish with finely sliced fresh chives and a pinch of flaky salt.
15. Serve immediately.

---

## Notes
- Yukon Golds produce a naturally buttery, creamy mash. Russets are fluffier and absorb more cream and butter. Both work well.
- Drying the potatoes out over heat after draining is an important step. Excess moisture makes the mash watery regardless of how much butter you add.
- Always add warm cream and butter to the potatoes, never cold. Cold dairy tightens the starch and produces a gluey texture.
- The herb simmer is the defining step. Do not rush it. The longer the aromatics steep, the more fragrant the final mash.
- Greek yogurt is optional but recommended. It cuts through the richness and keeps the mash from being one-dimensional.
- For cheese mashed potatoes, Gruyère adds nuttiness, sharp cheddar adds bite, and Parmesan adds salt and umami. A combination of two works well.
- These can be made ahead and kept warm in a double boiler or low oven. Stir in a splash of warm cream before serving to loosen if needed.
- Brown butter can substitute for or supplement the regular butter for a nuttier, more complex finish.
